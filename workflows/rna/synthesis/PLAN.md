# Synthesis of three independent cohort summaries

This is a local plan for the revision-stage reanalysis. It precedes inspection of the replacement signature associations. Cohorts are GSE234933 (one of the two recurrence specimens per donor, HN23 retained in the primary branch), GSE181919 (only primary cancers), and GSE310797 (16 independent oral-cancer patients). Denominators include source-file failures. Counts, author annotations and eligibility are audited in each cohort. Distinct source studies recruited at different centres; no common participant registry is available for cross-study linkage.

For each cohort use exactly the same primary endpoint and model. The first synthesis uses the equal-patient arithmetic mean of finite-cell observed correlations and its squared patient-level standard error on the raw correlation scale. The second uses the random-effects mean of the Fisher-transformed associations and its within-cohort classical HKSJ standard error on the Fisher scale. Keep the two estimands separate. Only the primary specimen selection, ungated cell set and depth-adjusted model enter the cross-cohort calculations. Never treat alternate branches, two estimators, or leave-one-out fits as independent studies.

Fit intercept-only random-effects models by restricted maximum likelihood within each synthesis. Weight by the reciprocal of the cohort sampling variance plus fitted between-cohort variance. For the mean use a two-sided t interval on K−1 degrees of freedom, with the Hartung–Knapp dispersion factor floored at one. Report Cochran's fixed-weight Q, I² = max(0,(Q−(K−1))/Q) when Q is positive (zero at Q=0), and the model-scale between-cohort variance. Use a two-sided model-based prediction interval for the corresponding mean of a comparable new cohort, with t on K−2 degrees of freedom and the square root of the sum of the mean's squared standard error and the between-cohort variance. Back-transform Fisher-scale estimates and endpoints. Keep native-scale limits without clipping. If K<3, do not report the three-cohort prediction interval. Show primary outcome leave-one-cohort-out mean estimates as sensitivity, and distinguish any source/signature derivation cohort if applicable. With three cohorts the prediction interval uses one degree of freedom; neither I² nor the interval precisely characterises heterogeneity.

For the same-endpoint cell pooling comparison report the unadjusted pooled-cell Pearson estimate, the Pearson estimate from stacked patient-specific depth residuals, and the equal-patient mean. For each cohort show the largest donor's share and change on removal of that donor. The largest contributor is selected by cell count before examination of correlations. A donor's leverage depends on residual variation as well as cell count. Differences in weighting, transformation, and target can change summaries even when no single donor dominates. Actual influence estimates, not a presumption of dominance, determine the wording.
