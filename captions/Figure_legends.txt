Figure 1. Patient-level association workflow. (A) Identical schematic cells are pooled or analysed within patients after depth adjustment; colours and shapes identify patients. (B) Equal-patient t, REML–HKSJ and empirical Bayes target the mean patient correlation, a population Fisher-scale mean and individual patient effects, respectively; equal point areas, weight-proportional square areas and shrinkage arrows illustrate the summaries, while dashed lines mark the labelled means and separate ticks mark zero. The report icons denote method-eligible patients, excluded patients and warnings; PASE 1.1.0 defaults to descriptive output below 6 eligible patients and warns below 20 or when the full interval width exceeds 0.2, with these configurable defaults remaining reporting choices rather than validated adequacy thresholds. (C) Benchmarks (Figures 2–5) and the application to 3 HNSCC cohorts (Figure 6) evaluate and illustrate the workflow; all geometry is schematic, and the summary positions in B are not calculated from the cells in A.

Figure 2. Patient clustering and unequal cell contributions. (A) Gaussian null controls use 8 patients and 2,000 cohorts per allocation; points and error bars show rejection rates and pointwise 95% Wilson intervals, and the dashed line marks 5%. (B) Cells from the largest patient are repeated tenfold with processed scores and patient identities held fixed; circles and diamonds show original and duplicated-cell estimates, joined by lines, for 48/51, 19/20 and 15/16 eligible/attempted patients in GSE234933, GSE181919 and GSE310797. (C) Each point represents one patient's myeloid-cell count before outcome-dependent gating, plotted on a logarithmic axis with a dashed line at 30 cells; circles indicate at least 30 cells and triangles indicate fewer than 30 cells. All 51 and 20 counts are shown in the first two cohorts, and 15 of 16 counts are available in GSE310797. The remaining GSE310797 patient, PT13, has an incomplete source count record and is retained in the patient list with the count marked unavailable; it is not represented as zero. Four known counts fall below 30 cells: 9, 26 and 29 in GSE234933 and 3 in GSE181919.

Figure 3. Calibration in the registered count simulation. Columns show coverage at latent means of 0 (A) and 0.1 (B), and Type I error under patient-wise random signs (C), with 6 patient counts, 5 effect distributions and 6 interval procedures; τ describes the distribution before sign randomisation. The 15-gene score uses observed-count normalisation, depth adjustment and no detection gate, with 2,000–16,000 independent cohorts per condition. Percentages are conditional on available intervals; equal-patient intervals target the eligible mean observed correlation, whereas Fisher procedures target the eligible marginal population Fisher mean. The colour-scale centres are 95% for coverage and 5% for Type I error, with saturated colours outside the displayed scale and labels retaining the actual percentages. Source data provide attempted and available counts, Monte Carlo bounds and joint coverage; the signed control is a zero-population-mean condition, not an individual-patient sharp null.

Figure 4. Informative cell yield and outcome-dependent gating. The left column (A, D, G) shows bias, root mean squared error and interval width for equal, lognormal and dominant cell allocation at J = 8 or 20 with 20% reversed effects; dominant allocation gives one patient half of all cells, and coupling links cell yield to effect strength. The middle and right columns (B, C, E, F, H, I) show gated-minus-ungated changes for MMP14 and the 15-gene score across 4 latent means, with τ = 0.1 and effects independent of yield. Colours distinguish equal-patient t and REML–HKSJ, while marker shapes and line types distinguish the two patient counts. Conditions contain 4,000–16,000 cohorts, and interval availability is 100% in the 84 displayed method–condition records; bias and error are evaluated against each method's own target. Left-column error bars show ±1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors; middle/right envelopes sum the component pointwise uncertainty half-widths, using reference bands for target changes. All bias-change envelopes in E and F include zero; these envelopes describe Monte Carlo uncertainty and are not paired or simultaneous confidence intervals.

Figure 5. Method comparisons and computational requirements. (A) Gaussian rejection rates use 8 patients and 2,000 cohorts per setting, with dark and light grey distinguishing equal allocation of 80 cells per patient from unequal yields of 20–260 cells. (B) Bars show rejection counts among 999 within-patient LILRB4–MMP14 shuffles in GSE181919, using 19 of 20 primary-tumour patients; the excluded patient had 3 myeloid cells, and the memento shuffle experiment was not run. Pseudobulk means are unchanged by these permutations, so the zero count does not measure calibration of a within-patient association test. (C) Lines show median elapsed time over 3 successful runs at each cell count for 1 focal pair among 32 genes from 20 patients; PASE includes its cohort and individual summaries, while memento includes patient moments without bootstrap tests. (D) Native count scenarios use 8 patients with 80 cells each and 500 cohorts per scenario; all cohort tests were available, while memento retained 2–8 patients depending on the scenario. In A and D, intervals are pointwise 95% Wilson intervals and dashed lines mark the nominal 5% rate in null panels; axis limits differ between null and alternative scenarios, whose tests concern method-specific targets. Table S5 reports the full reference-pair counts, shuffle counts, runtimes and memory measurements.

Figure 6. Patient-level associations in 3 RNA cohorts. (A) Individual points show ungated, depth-adjusted patient correlations at the 30-cell threshold, alongside equal-patient means with t intervals and back-transformed Fisher-scale REML–HKSJ summaries; the 3 cohorts contribute 48/51, 19/20 and 15/16 eligible/attempted patients. Patient-point area represents contributing cell count rather than inferential weight; the 2 cohort summaries retain their distinct arithmetic-correlation and Fisher-scale targets. (B) Cohort estimates and 95% intervals are shown at the 10-, 30- and 50-cell thresholds; the corresponding eligible counts are 50/48/48, 19/19/17 and 15/15/15. (C) Gated-minus-ungated mean differences and pointwise 95% paired t intervals use the 48, 18 and 15 patients eligible in both branches. The q values adjust the 3 two-sided comparisons by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure.
